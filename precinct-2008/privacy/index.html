Precinct 2008 privacy policy

iPhone, iPad · Private beta (TestFlight) · Last updated 2026-10-29

A field guide and poll worker roster for one precinct's election inspector, all stored on the iPhone and working without a signal.

What this app is

Precinct 2008 is a reference app for the inspector of one polling place. It holds a bundled 226-page poll worker guide that you can search, checklists, a list of key facts, a schedule, and a roster where the inspector keeps their poll workers' names, phone numbers, tags, notes and checkboxes. It also has a Notable people list for anyone else the inspector may need to reach, and a free-text Notes area. Everything is built into the app or saved on the phone, so it works in airplane mode.

The app is not a voting tool. It handles no voter information and gives no voting or legal advice. It only shows the guide and the checklists it contains and keeps the inspector's own notes. It is in private TestFlight testing and is not publicly released.

Diagnostic and usage data

Precinct 2008 keeps a diagnostic log on your phone, in the app's own storage. It writes a line every 10 seconds while the app is running. Each line says whether the app is in the foreground or background, which screen is open, how many people are on the roster, how many checklist items are ticked, the memory in use, the battery level, the free storage space, and whether the last save failed. It also records app opening and closing, tab and screen changes, filter and tag taps, checklist and checkbox ticks, which guide page you opened, and counts for backups, imports, exports and erasing.

The log is designed not to contain names, phone numbers, email addresses, notes or what you type. Text is kept only for a short list of fixed labels used by the app itself, and every other piece of text is reduced to its length. Searches and questions are logged only as a count of results and a count of words. The app does not log scrolling, pointer movement or typed text.

The log is not uploaded. The app has no code that sends anything over the internet. Apart from an iPhone, iCloud or computer backup of the phone, the log leaves the phone only if the developer exports it from the app. Log files older than 30 days are deleted when the app opens, and erasing the app's data removes the existing log files (the app then starts a new log that holds only counts). A short line about the system activity, the type of event only, also goes to Apple's system log on the phone.

What it does not collect

  • No advertising and no tracking
  • No analytics or crash-reporting tools from other companies, and no other third-party code
  • No accounts or sign-in
  • No internet connection: the app contains no code to send or receive data
  • No access to your contacts, camera, microphone, photos or location (the app does not read your Contacts app; Notable people is its own list)
  • No automatic upload of the roster, notes or logs
  • No voter or ballot information

How long it keeps data, and how to delete it

Your roster, notable people, notes, checkboxes, schedule changes and checklist ticks stay on the phone until you remove them. Removing a person from the roster deletes that person and their notes from the phone. Deleting a notable person removes them from the phone. Deleting a default checkbox in Settings removes it, and everyone's ticks on it, after asking you first. You get 8 seconds to undo it, and the undo is kept in memory only. Backups made before the removal still contain that person until those backups are deleted; the newest 30 are kept, and Erase All Data deletes all backups saved on the phone. The newest 30 backups are kept on the phone, and you can delete any one of them by swiping on it in Settings. Log files are deleted after 30 days.

To delete everything the app holds, open Settings, then Erase All Data on This iPhone, and confirm. This deletes the roster, notable people, the Notes area, checkboxes, checklist ticks and schedule changes, the backups saved on the phone, the existing log files, the app's settings and the temporary export files. The welcome screen then shows again, as on a new install.

Erasing does not touch the backup folder you picked, files you exported and shared, or iPhone backups made by iCloud or your computer. Delete those yourself. Deleting the app from the phone also removes its data on the phone, but not those copies.

Screenshots added to a message are deleted when that message is deleted, when the app finds nothing that refers to them any more, and by Erase All Data. Because screenshots are included in backups, backups made earlier still contain them until those backups are deleted.

Children

Precinct 2008 is not made for children. It has no accounts, no ads and no tracking, and it does not send information about anyone to the developer. The roster is whatever the inspector types or imports. If it includes younger poll workers, such as the Student Clerk role, their details stay on the phone in the same way as everyone else's.
