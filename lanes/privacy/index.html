Lanes privacy policy

iPhone, Mac, Android, web · Private beta (TestFlight) · Last updated 2026-10-23

A personal dashboard for people who run several Claude Code projects on their own Mac.

What this app is

Lanes is a personal developer dashboard. It shows how full each project's task list is, what is waiting on the owner, and a chat screen for their Claude Code chats. It talks only to a small server that the owner runs on their own Mac, and a second copy on the owner's own always-on Mac.

There are no accounts, no email and no passwords. Pair with the setup link or QR code the owner gives you. Today the only user is the developer, and the app is on a private TestFlight beta.

Diagnostic and usage data

Lanes records what happens in the app so problems can be found and fixed. On iPhone and Mac, it writes a heartbeat line every 10 seconds, even when idle, with whether the device is online, the network type (Wi-Fi, cellular or Ethernet), memory use, CPU use, free disk space, battery level and whether it is charging, heat state and low-power mode. It also records every tap, click, scroll, key press, focus change and screen change, pointer movement about four times a second, network calls (the method, address, result and time taken), errors, and when the app starts, stops, sleeps or wakes.

What you type is designed not to be recorded. For a typed field the log is built to keep only the field's name and how long the text is, and secure fields show as dots. Secrets are masked in the logs.

Each line carries the time, the device name, the app and version, and a random session number. Logs are kept on the device and uploaded every few seconds to the developer's own server, which is the same server the app is paired with. They are not sent to any analytics or advertising company.

The Android app is simpler. It writes a heartbeat every 10 seconds (the screen, network type, memory, battery level and free disk space) and a line for taps, actions and errors into a log file in the app's own storage on the phone. It does not record pointer movement, and to our knowledge it is not uploaded, so it stays on the phone. It is not deleted automatically; clear it by clearing the app's storage in Android settings.

On iPhone and Mac, logs are kept for 30 days on the device (up to about 1 GB) and for 30 days on the server, then deleted. Hang and crash reports on the server are kept for up to 60 days.

What it does not collect

  • No tracking and no tracking domains
  • No advertising and no advertising identifier
  • No analytics, crash-reporting or advertising software
  • No accounts, email addresses, passwords, names or phone numbers
  • No camera, photo-library, contacts, location or Bluetooth access
  • No payments or purchases
  • No claude.ai login inside Lanes; Lanes never sees your claude.ai sign-in
  • The text you type is designed not to be recorded in the usage logs

How long it keeps data, and how to delete it

On iPhone and Mac: go to Settings and choose Erase All Data on This Device. It lists what goes (the server and pairing, settings and drafts, saved data and logs), says in bold that your Mac and other devices are not touched, and then asks you to confirm. The app then behaves like a fresh install. Deleting the app has the same effect. The web version has no erase screen; clear your browser's site data instead. On Android, Settings has Forget this server, which removes the saved server and key from the phone; there is no Erase All Data screen, so also clear the app's storage in Android settings to remove its log. Android device backups are turned off for the app.

Erasing does not contact the server. On iPhone and Mac, logs on the developer's own server are deleted after 30 days, hang and crash reports after 60 days, files you attached are removed from temporary storage after 1 hour, and the last 500 notifications are kept on the owner's Mac. A push token that Apple reports as no longer valid is dropped automatically.

Children

Lanes is not directed at children. It is a tool for developers. It does not ask for or collect age, birth date or any child-specific information.
